# Piper: complete instructions

Piper is an animated math teacher for grades 6 to 8. She talks, shows emotion, reads photos of problems and student work, and remembers each learner. These instructions are for the adult who sets her up and sits nearby.

App link (private until you share it): https://claude.ai/artifact/7hWNGJQnaybRBBqh1u2Nnf

## 1. What you need
- A claude.ai account (the adult's). Every answer Piper gives uses this account's Claude usage.
- A computer, tablet or Chromebook for the main screen. Best voice quality: Microsoft Edge on Windows, or Safari/Chrome on an iPad or Mac.
- Optional: a phone with a camera, signed in to claude.ai with the same account, for sending photos.
- Speakers or headphones if Piper will talk.

## 2. First-time setup (about 10 minutes)
1. Open the app link on the main screen while signed in to claude.ai.
2. Click **How it works** at the top to see the in-app guide.
3. Fill in **Piper's Notebook** on the right (below the chat on a phone). The most important field is **What helps me learn**: write accommodations in plain words, such as "pictures first, one step at a time, calculator OK for computation, no timers, read problems aloud." Leave out diagnoses, last names and school names.
4. Click **Save notebook**.
5. Set up the voice (section 4), then click **Test voice**.
6. Optional: on the phone, scan the QR code once now (section 5) so the phone is already signed in.

## 3. The first session: getting to know each other
If the notebook has no nickname, Piper starts with a short conversation before any math. She asks, one at a time:
1. What to call the learner
2. Grade or math class
3. What they love doing (she uses this in word problems)
4. How math feels right now, with five tap buttons from 😣 "I hate it" to 😄 "I love it"
5. One time math went OK, even something small
6. What makes math hard and what helps (the adult can add here)
7. Whether she should talk out loud

At the end she summarizes, saves the notebook automatically, and asks whether to start with homework or a warm-up. To run this conversation again later, click **Redo intro chat**.

The first time Piper answers, claude.ai asks permission for the page to use Claude. Choose **Allow**. If you decline, reload the page to be asked again.

## 4. Voice: talking and listening
**Piper talking**
1. Click **🔊 Piper talks** so it is dark (on). Every answer is read aloud and her mouth moves.
2. Under **Piper's voice**, choose a voice and speed. Piper picks the most natural female voice the device has, but the choice depends on the device:
   - Windows: use Microsoft Edge and pick a voice with "Natural" in its name (for example Aria or Jenny).
   - iPhone, iPad, Mac: go to Settings, Accessibility, Spoken Content (Mac: System Settings, Accessibility, Spoken Content), Voices, English, and download an **Enhanced** or **Premium** voice such as Ava or Samantha. Reload the page, then pick it.
   - Chromebook or Android: pick a "Google" voice.
3. Any single answer can be replayed with **Read aloud** under it.
4. If a yellow notice appears under the voice list, the chosen voice is likely to sound robotic; follow the fix it shows for that device. Each time you choose a voice, Piper says a short sentence so you can compare.
5. Click **Find her best voice** for a guided setup: it shows the steps for this device, then lets you hear the top voices say the same lines in Piper's calm, caring and cheering tones. Click **Choose** on the one you like.
6. Piper's tone follows her face: slower and softer when she shows empathy, brighter when she celebrates, with a short breath between sentences.
7. For a human-quality voice on every device, see [VOICE-UPGRADE.md](VOICE-UPGRADE.md).

**The learner talking**
The page cannot use the microphone directly, so the learner speaks through the device's dictation:
- Phone or tablet: tap the box, then the 🎤 on the keyboard, speak, and press **Send**.
- Windows: click the box, press **Windows key + H**, speak, press **Send**.
- Mac: click the box, press **Fn** (or the Globe key) twice, speak, press **Send**.
- Chromebook: turn on dictation in Settings, Accessibility, Keyboard and text input, then press **Search + D**.

The **🎤 Talk** button in the app shows these steps.

## 5. Sending a photo from a phone (QR code)
1. On the main screen, click **📱 Phone photo**. A QR code appears.
2. Open the phone's camera, point it at the code, and tap the link that pops up.
3. If asked, sign in to claude.ai on the phone **with the same account** as the main screen.
4. Tap **📷 Take a photo**, photograph the problem (and the learner's work if they tried it). Or tap **🖼️ Choose from my photos**.
5. Check the preview, then tap **Send to Piper**.
6. Within a few seconds the photo appears on the main screen, Piper looks surprised, a chime plays, and she starts reading it.

Tips for good photos: the whole problem in frame, bright light, no shadow, phone held flat above the paper. Piper always reads the problem back first so you can correct any misread number.

The phone page can stay open: send as many photos as you like. Photos are private to your account and are deleted from storage as soon as the main screen receives them. A photo older than 15 minutes is ignored.

If the QR code doesn't show, copy the link under it (**Copy link**) and open it on the phone.

You can also skip the phone: **📷 Photo** on the main screen uploads a picture from that device.

## 6. Piper's faces, sounds and movement
| Face | When | Movement and sound |
|---|---|---|
| Hello | Page opens | Waves |
| Calm | Ready to help | Gentle breathing, blinking, slight sway |
| Listening | The learner is typing | Head tilted toward them |
| Thinking | Working on an answer | Hand on chin, eyes up, thought bubbles |
| Empathy | Frustration, worry, "I'm dumb", "I hate math" | Soft eyebrows, head tilt, pulsing heart, soft low tone |
| Encourage | The learner keeps trying | Nods |
| Curious | Asking how they thought about it | Head tilt, raised eyebrow |
| Happy | A good step | Big smile |
| Celebrate | A real win (also added to the Wins jar) | Bounces, waves, sparkles, chime |
| Surprised | A photo arrives | Raised eyebrows, open mouth, chime |

Piper reacts with empathy the moment a frustrated message is sent, before her answer arrives. Turn sounds off with **🔔 Sounds**. If the device is set to reduce motion, the movement stops and only the faces change.

## 7. During a session
- **Quick buttons**: I'm stuck, Show me a picture, Check my answer, Practice one, I need a break. Good for learners who find typing hard.
- **Answer buttons** appear under some of Piper's questions; the learner can tap one or type their own answer.
- **New problem** clears the photo and starts fresh.
- **Bigger text** enlarges and spaces out the text, which helps with dyslexia and low vision.
- Piper asks one question at a time, never hands over answers, and goes back to the first wrong step in the learner's work.

## 8. After a session
1. Click **Update from today**. Piper rewrites the notebook: what was mastered, tricky spots, how math feels now, new wins, and where to start next time.
2. Read it over and edit anything that isn't right, then **Save notebook**.
3. Once a week, read the **Wins jar** and **How math feels** with the learner. Seeing progress is a large part of rebuilding how they feel about math.

## 9. How the adult can help
- Be curious alongside the learner: "I don't know, let's figure it out" helps more than "I was never good at math either" (parents' math anxiety can pass to kids, Maloney et al., 2015).
- Praise the strategy ("you drew a picture first, smart move") rather than speed or being "smart."
- Before a test, let the learner say what worries them for a minute.
- If Piper misreads a photo, say so; she will correct herself.

## 10. Privacy and safety
- The notebook and phone photos are stored privately for the signed-in account, with a backup copy of the notebook in the browser.
- Piper only uses a nickname. Keep last names, school names, faces and medical details out of the notebook and photos.
- If the learner says they are hurt or unsafe, Piper stops the math and tells them to talk to the adult nearby; in the US they can also call or text 988.
- The link is private. Use the page's Share menu only for adults who will supervise.

## 11. Troubleshooting
| Problem | Fix |
|---|---|
| "Piper can't reach Claude from here" | Open the link on claude.ai while signed in; reload and choose Allow |
| Piper sounds robotic | Pick a Natural, Enhanced or Premium voice (section 4), or use Edge on Windows |
| No voices listed | Reload the page; on some browsers voices load a few seconds late |
| Phone photo never arrives | Both devices must be signed in to the same claude.ai account; keep the main screen's page open; send again |
| QR code missing | Use Copy link and open it on the phone |
| "Saved in this browser only" | You are not signed in, or the page was opened outside claude.ai |
| Piper rushes or gives long answers | Add "one step at a time, short messages" to What helps me learn |
| Chat feels slow | Normal for longer thinking; photos take longer than text |

## 12. Using Piper without the app
The `piper-math-tutor` folder is a Claude skill with the same teaching approach. Upload `dist/piper-math-tutor.zip` in claude.ai under Settings, Capabilities, Skills, then say "Hi Piper, I need help with math." The skill has no animated face and no phone QR code, but it uses the same pedagogy, intro conversation and notebook.
